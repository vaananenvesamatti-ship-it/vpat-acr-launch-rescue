Your customer asked for a VPAT.

Know what to test, what to document, what you can safely claim, and what to do next — without spending days piecing the process together.

Who this is for: a 5–200 person SaaS or software team that has just been asked for a VPAT or Accessibility Conformance Report by a customer, university, enterprise procurement team, or government buyer — and has no accessibility specialist on staff.

The situation

A deal is moving. Procurement sends a line item asking for your VPAT or ACR. Nobody on the team has produced one before.

So the questions start. Which of the four editions did they actually mean? What counts as "the product" — just the app, or onboarding and the help centre too? Can we write Supports for something we believe is fine but never tested? What happens if we get it wrong?

You did not plan to spend this week becoming an accessibility documentation expert. You planned to close the deal.

What this is

A downloadable application that turns the request into a worked process: it tells you what to test, keeps the evidence next to each claim, and shows you what is still missing before you send anything.

What it does
OrientationWork out what was actually requested, which edition applies, and what is in scope — before you start testing.
Guided testingFor each criterion, what to check and what counts as adequate evidence.
Evidence bindingA positive conformance claim cannot be recorded without the test behind it: method, date, result, reference, scope.
Reusable evidenceOne keyboard-navigation test can support several criteria at once, applied in a single action.
Gap detectionUnsupported claims, untested criteria, incomplete sections, and claims resting on automated scans alone.
Readiness summaryWhether the project is ready for internal review, still needs testing, or should go to a specialist.
Runs locallyNo account, no upload, no server. Your product's accessibility findings stay on your machine.
ExportStructured project data and an evidence register you keep, so the next version is not a fresh start.

Why not just ask ChatGPT?

Ask it, genuinely — it explains VPATs well, and that part is free.

What it will not do is refuse you. Ask a model to draft a conformance claim and it drafts one, because that is what you asked for. It does not know what you tested, does not keep the evidence, does not remember the project next week, and does not tell you that eleven of your claims have nothing behind them.

The difference is not knowledge. It is structure and state.

Why not hire a specialist immediately?

Sometimes you should, and this tool will tell you when it thinks so.

But a specialist engagement starts faster and costs less when you arrive with scope defined, testing already done where you could do it, and evidence organised — instead of paying someone to work that out. Plenty of requests also turn out narrower than they first looked. This helps you find out which situation you are in.

What this is not.

  • It does not certify accessibility, and no self-produced ACR does.
  • It does not guarantee compliance with any law or standard.
  • It does not replace a qualified audit where one is genuinely required.
  • It is not legal advice.
  • It cannot guarantee that a procurement team will accept your ACR.
  • It does not make an inaccessible product accessible. It documents what is true.
